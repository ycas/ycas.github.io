<b>One Target. Four Collaborators. Nearly 58 Hours of Data.
<br>YCAS Astrophotography Collaboration - NGC 2237, the Rosette Nebula</b> <br><br>Astrophotography is often thought of as a solitary pursuit-one photographer, one telescope, and one image. The members of the <b>York County Astronomical Society (YCAS)</b> decided to take a different approach. <br>Four YCAS astrophotographers-<b>Don, Ken, Adam, and Julie</b>-worked together over multiple nights to capture <b>NGC 2237, the Rosette Nebula</b>. Using different telescopes, cameras, mounts, filters, and imaging techniques, the group collected nearly <b>58 hours of combined data</b>, bringing their individual contributions together to create a single composite image. 
<br><br>The project demonstrates what can be accomplished when astrophotographers combine their equipment, experience, and data toward a common target. 
<br><br>

<br><br><b>Combining Different Approaches</b>
<br>The strength of this project came from combining data captured with very different imaging systems. The group brought together <b>Hα, OIII, and SII</b> data from multiple telescopes and cameras, along with color information from the OSC systems.
<br><br>
<b>Don and Ken contributed OIII data, while Don contributed SII, and Adam and Julie supplied Hα data</b>. The resulting datasets provided the full range of wavelengths for a complete image of the Rosette&#39;s complex emission structures and gave the group greater flexibility during processing. <br><br>To showcase the different paths for processing the group took the collated data and processed the data separately. Allowing each contributor to use all of the data to create the artistic profile they preferred. Whether it be the HOO or SHO each image is truly stunning.

The picture of the Heart Nebula (IC1805) was taken between Dec 24 and Jan 25. Comprising six nights of imagining. Adam used the GTI71 with the ASI 2600MC Astrophotography Camera. Utilizing narrowband filters, Adam captured about 9.5 hours using the Askar D1 (HaOiii) filter and about 9.5 hours using the Asker D2 (S2Oiii) filter. The images were stacked and processed using Pixinsight using the Hubble SHO Pallet.

![YCAS Logo](../img/LN/Leo3.jpg "Leo Triplet")
The Leo Triplet of galaxies consists of three galaxies, which lie at an average distance of 35 million light-years from Earth, below the belly of the constellation Leo the lion. NGC 3628 (top-left in the image) is viewed edge-on, while Messier 65 (bottom-right) and Messier 66 (top-right) are tilted towards the Earth. M65 &amp; 66 were discovered by Charles Messier on March 1, 1780; however, his telescope lacked the resolution to detect the dimmest of the trio, NGC 3628. Also known as the Hamburger Galaxy, NGC 3628 was not discovered until April 8, 1784, by William Herschel.

The image was captured from Wallingford-MAK Observatory on February 20, 2021. It consists of 125 2-minute exposures for a total integration time of just over four hours. The data was captured with SGP with pre and postprocessing performed in Pixinsight and Photoshop (watermark).

Found in the constellation Auriga, the Tadpole is located 12,000 light-years from Earth, and is about 100 light-years across. The Flaming Star nebula is located 1,500 from Earth and approximately five light-years across.
